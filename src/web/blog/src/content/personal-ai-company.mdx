Running a company on your own means doing jobs you've never had to do before, usually with nobody around to ask. Even when you know your craft well, there are only so many hours in the day to learn everything else, and hiring a full team is often out of reach.

Spinning up a few AI agents looks like an easy way to get help. Pretty quickly, though, you find yourself repeating the same background across separate chats, copying one agent's output into another window, and checking whether anyone picked up the next step. Instead of clearing your plate, you've added one more job: managing the agents.

For your own agent company, the practical goal is a team that hands work directly to each other and brings you what still needs a decision. Below, we walk through four starting roles and a sample customer job from start to finish, including how to set them up in a [multi agent collaboration workspace](https://alook.ai).

## What Should Your Team Know About Your Business?

Say you run a website-maintenance service for local shops, updating opening hours and fixing broken links on existing sites.

When a shop asks for a brand-new website, that sounds like a great lead on the surface, except it's a different service altogether. If Sales treats routine maintenance and full redesigns the same way, you end up discussing work you haven't priced or planned to deliver.

A **company brief** gives Sales a clear reference for what you offer, who it's for, and when to pull you in. A **job brief** holds one customer's agreed task, like which pages to edit, the updated hours, and the confirmed booking URL. You can reuse the company brief whenever you add a new agent, while customer-specific details stay attached to that customer's job.

## How Many Agents Do You Need to Start?

For a business like this, four roles make a strong starting point: Sales, Delivery, Review, and Customer Ops. Chasing new leads and tending to active jobs pull attention in opposite directions, and every website change needs a second pair of eyes before a client sees it.

Review is the one role we'd resist folding into Delivery. An agent grading its own work tends to trust its own summary and miss the exact mistake it was supposed to catch. A separate Reviewer compares the customer's original request against the actual preview.

Four isn't a hard minimum for every business. Early on, one agent can cover both Sales and Customer Ops. Once prospect research starts slowing down replies to existing customers, split them up. Just don't add extra agents solely to make the company look bigger.

## How to Structure AI Agents Into Departments and Roles

### Give Each Role Something You Can Check

“Sales” could mean researching local leads, or it could mean quoting prices to buyers. Defining concrete deliverables upfront lets you catch that gap before a real customer gets the wrong promise.

Sales handles prospect research and incoming inquiries first. If outbound research starts delaying replies to people ready to buy, split Prospecting from Intake. Prospecting passes lead notes to Intake so it has context when handling inquiries and drafting job briefs. Both still report to you, and one summarizes sales activity for Customer Ops so you don't wake up to two more chat threads every morning.

### Reporting Lines and Direct Handoffs

All four agents report to you, yet you shouldn't have to sit in the middle of every handoff:

**Sales → Delivery → Review → Customer Ops**

When Review catches a broken link, Delivery gets that feedback directly without waiting on you to copy it over. Customer Ops pulls progress and open questions into one summary, saving you from polling four separate bots for status updates.

Promises to customers and changes in scope are where you draw firm lines. Customer Ops can pass a typo or hour correction back to Delivery, while a request for a brand-new page goes back to Sales for a quote. You set every price and approve every scope change. Passing review doesn't push changes live automatically, either: publishing, invoices, and payment calls always wait for you.

Vague prompts like “continue” often leave two agents politely waiting on each other. Naming the next agent and the exact step to pick up removes the guesswork. Even complex [agent orchestration](/blog/ai-agent-orchestration) comes down to getting that handoff clear.

### Set Each Agent's Tool Access

Delivery needs a staging space where it can prepare edits and share a preview link with Review. Neither agent needs permission to publish to production just to do those jobs. Broad publish access makes it way too easy for an agent to update a live site while you're still checking the preview.

The same applies to Sales and Customer Ops: drafting a customer email doesn't require permission to send it. Spell out which accounts each role may touch and whether messages wait for your review, then enforce those boundaries in the tools themselves. Writing “don't publish” in a role prompt won't take away access to a live publish button.

## Run a Customer Job Through the Team

### Sales Hands Over the Job Brief

Suppose a shop asks for updated opening hours and a fixed booking link, without sending the replacement URL. Sales should flag that gap in the draft job brief right away so Delivery doesn't waste time guessing which booking page is right.

Once you approve the brief, Sales hands it off to Delivery, which replies quickly to confirm it's on the task. If the customer messages later to change Saturday's hours, update the job brief itself instead of letting a loose correction get buried in chat scrollback.

We ran into this exact version drift in our own article workflow. Even when we sent both a draft and its version number to a reviewing agent, we still got comments pointing at lines we'd already rewritten. We had to stop and check which draft the reviewer actually read before fixing anything. Delivery hits the same snag whenever a customer's latest message doesn't match the brief it started from.

### Send the Website Preview for Review

![Six handoff requirements: scope, input, output, completion criteria, failure path, and approval boundary](/blog/personal-ai-company/handoff-contract.webp)

For Delivery, the shop's job brief spells out both the work and what to hand back:

<CompanyJobBrief>

| Field | This customer's job |
|---|---|
| Scope | Change the hours and booking link; keep the existing design |
| Input | Affected pages, correct hours, and confirmed booking URL |
| Output | Preview link, change list, and results of checking the hours and booking link |
| Completion | Each requested change is ready to check, or the team has said exactly what's missing |
| Failure | Ask Customer Ops for missing details or help with access; keep the work already done and bring scope questions to you |
| Approval | Prepare and revise the preview; publish only after you agree |

</CompanyJobBrief>

Review has to actually open that preview and verify that the booking button opens the URL in the job brief. Taking Delivery's summary of “link fixed” at face value misses the whole point of independent review.

Feedback like “looks wrong” just gives Delivery another guessing game. A useful review note sounds like: “The booking link still opens the old destination. Use the URL in job brief v2, then return the updated preview and link-check result.” On the next pass, Review checks that exact link again.

### When the Job Gets Stuck

A missing booking URL doesn't stop Delivery from finishing the rest of the work. Delivery updates the hours first and tells Customer Ops: “Hours ready in preview; booking link waiting for the confirmed destination.” That gives Customer Ops one clear question for the client. If Customer Ops isn't allowed to email clients directly yet, it queues that question up for you to send.

Once the URL is confirmed and Review signs off, Customer Ops brings you the preview and check results for final approval. If the customer spots a wrong closing hour afterward, that fix goes back through another quick check. A request for a whole new page starts a separate conversation about price and scope.

## Keep Customer Jobs Moving

Once two or three shops are active at the same time, it's easy for a booking URL or reply to end up on the wrong client's job. Keep every customer job in its own brief and conversation, and make sure any agent picking up a task reads that customer's history first.

Each morning, you want a short digest from Customer Ops: inquiries waiting on a price, previews ready for sign-off, jobs waiting on client info, and overdue follow-ups. That lets you step in where needed without reading every message, while Delivery and Review keep moving on approved tasks.

Items waiting on your sign-off need to stay visible in that list too, so a quiet thread never looks like a finished job.

## Is the Team Saving You Time?

The clearest early test is how often work bounces back to you asking for context you already gave. If Delivery still needs you to piece the customer's request back together by hand, adding more agents won't fix a broken handoff.

Count the time you spend reviewing, correcting mistakes, and chasing follow-ups right alongside the hands-on website edits. Model and tool bills count too. A handful of completed jobs will tell you much more than a fast first draft, and if you don't have earlier numbers, your next few jobs will give you a solid baseline.

When an agent keeps returning work you can't use, give it a [narrower task](/blog/how-to-delegate-tasks-to-ai-agents) first. Spinning up another role before fixing that one just leaves you supervising one more agent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Does “Zero-Person Company” Mean Here?

Here we mean one human owner running a business with an AI agent team. Terms like “zero-person company” or “0-person company” tend to overstate how much you can leave on autopilot. In practice, you still approve prices, scope changes, and publishing.

### How Is This Different From Claude Code Agent Teams?

Claude Code agent teams is a specific feature for coordinating multiple Claude Code sessions with a lead, shared tasks, and messaging. For the company in this guide, you organize ongoing business roles across your choice of tools, and the agents don't all have to run in Claude Code.
