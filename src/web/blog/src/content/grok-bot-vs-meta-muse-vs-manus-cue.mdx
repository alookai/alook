In everyday personal admin, Muse fits jobs like pulling dates out of school emails onto your calendar or keeping a recurring shopping list. You can drop a one-off question into a side chat without interrupting active Goals.

Muse covers small-business work too. Meta lists connectors including Shopify, QuickBooks, Slack, and Notion, letting a shop owner keep customer records and campaign drafts in one setup.

Grok Bot works well when you separate jobs into named roles. A purchasing Bot can use its own Bot email to request supplier quotes, then place orders using payment access under your approval rules. That stays separate from a research Bot writing your weekly competitor report.

Cue splits a broader goal across multiple agents in a group thread. On a trip, one agent can message venues while another organizes the itinerary, posting results back into the group so the next agent can pick up the handoff.

## Weekly Usage Limits: Upgrade or Split the Work?

Message counts tell you almost nothing about usage burn when an agent opens websites, reads files, and retries failed steps. Your weekly allowance has to cover the entire path to a usable result, including setup, trial runs, and follow-up edits.

Say you run a weekly research brief. A cheap first pass gets expensive fast if you spend three more runs fixing broken sources and unusable claims. Count those retries as part of the job’s real budget.

Shared pools need extra care. Because Grok’s weekly pool covers Grok Bot, Cursor, and Grok apps together, quick lookups and work elsewhere in the bundle can quietly eat into the allowance you needed for a deadline. Lock down one narrow recurring routine before jumping to a higher plan.

Splitting tasks across two products is often simpler than upgrading one. You can leave light daily admin in Muse and save Grok Bot for a heavier work role. You assign jobs in two places and copy results across when needed, while your demanding routine gets its own predictable budget.

## Multi-Agent Collaboration and Team Sharing

Grok Bot and Cue both show named agents handing tasks off to each other in chat, while Muse runs internal subagents (including a browser subagent) behind your main thread. If you work with teammates, check early who can join a conversation and which connected accounts they can reach.

Grok’s Shared Bots let Cursor teammates with Grok Bot access use the same configured role in group chats or Slack while keeping each person’s 1:1 chat private. Keep one security detail in mind: anyone using a Shared Bot can act through the tools, files, and secrets its owner connected, so only attach accounts you want teammates using. Replies draw from the allowance of whichever teammate the Bot answers, and Cursor team seats are priced separately from the individual plans above.

Cue’s group chat is built around dividing a goal among your own named agents. In its published travel example, agents post an itinerary, a packing list, and a booking handoff right beside the messages passing work to the next agent.

## Computer Access, Permissions and Payment Approvals

Decide upfront which purchases and outbound messages must pause for your approval.

Grok Bot lets you set approval rules per action. Note that saving a payment card does not guarantee a fresh confirmation prompt at every checkout, so check your purchase rules carefully. Running commands on your local desktop uses a separate permission that asks each time by default.

Muse handles permissions through Sentinel, which can allow, deny, or ask depending on the action and what you have already approved. When Sentinel requires approval, Muse pauses work until you answer. Because low-risk or previously approved actions can run without another prompt, pay attention to both the scope and duration of any permission you grant.

## What to Do When an AI Agent Gets Stuck

Hitting a usage cap, losing an expired login, and getting blocked by anti-bot checks need different fixes. Re-sending the same prompt usually wastes quota without clearing the blocker.

A usage stop is a budget choice: wait for the weekly reset or pay to finish the job. Login and verification prompts just need you to step in briefly and sign back in. And if a site blocks automated checkout, take the agent’s researched shortlist and complete the purchase yourself.

## Which AI Agent Should You Choose?

Split the work when your tasks naturally fit different setups: daily personal follow-ups in Muse, a repeatable work role in Grok Bot, or a multi-step goal shared across Cue’s named agents. Switch products altogether when how a tool organizes rooms or permissions keeps getting in your way, even when usage limits aren’t the issue.

If an agent already finishes real work with the accounts you’ve connected, weigh the price of the next plan tier against the hassle of rebuilding that setup elsewhere. Paying more makes sense when the upgrade covers the job at a cost you can justify.

Pick one recurring task next week and run it all the way through to an output you actually use.
