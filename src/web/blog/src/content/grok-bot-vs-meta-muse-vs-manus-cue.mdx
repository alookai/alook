## Grok Bot vs Meta Muse vs Manus Cue: What features do they offer?

You can use all three for work that continues beyond a chat reply. Their interfaces organize that work differently: Grok around Bot responsibilities, Muse around ongoing goals, and Cue around agents with separate identities and roles.

### Grok Bot: persistent roles and a shared team option

Give a Grok Bot a name and a job, such as research, outbound sales or expense management, then connect the services it needs. It keeps context over time and can pass work to other Bots. [Grok's overview](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/overview) describes the product separately from the Grok chatbot and Grok Build coding tool.

### Meta Muse: ongoing personal and business goals

Muse learns your preferences as it pursues goals over time. Keep the main conversation for ongoing work. Open side chats for separate topics. Meta's [design explanation](https://introducing.muse.ai/) puts goals and activity beside the conversation. It also shows suggested tasks, so you can check what Muse is doing while you are elsewhere.

### Manus Cue: separate agent identities and group handoffs

Each Cue agent gets its own email, phone number, wallet and computer; give several agents different responsibilities and put them in a group chat. The [Manus 2.0 announcement](https://www.manus.im/blog/introducing-manus-2-0) describes Cue as a standalone app. Check Cue-specific documentation before assuming it includes a feature from Manus Studio.

## App integrations and computer access: What can each agent use?

Grok Bot combines a cloud computer with authorized local commands. If you need Mac apps or business connectors, Muse supports both alongside its cloud environment. Cue provides an agent computer and connected services. Check your required accounts and files against each setup.

Suppose your customer briefing needs a CRM, email and a project folder. Use that list to check the documentation. We have not run this task as a comparison test.

## Team Bots and group chat: How does collaboration work?

In Grok Bot, you can use personal multi-Bot conversations or share a Team Bot. Cue shows agents dividing work in a group chat. Muse has side chats and business connections; the reviewed sources leave its multi-owner arrangement unclear. Check which people and accounts need to join.

- Grok Bot: A [Team Bot](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/team-bots) gives teammates a shared role and expertise, including access through Slack. **Shared team memory and personal notes have separate treatment.** Check the shared configuration and credentials; publishing a Team Bot differs from sharing a template someone copies.
- Meta Muse: [Side chats](https://introducing.muse.ai/) separate topics. Business connectors provide service access. If several owners need to participate, ask for a demonstration with their actual accounts; those features alone do not establish that arrangement.
- Manus Cue: The [official group-chat image](https://cue.im/) shows an itinerary agent handing work to a booking agent and a packing agent contributing a file. Named speakers and an @mention are visible. You can also see separate deliverables. The image does not establish every multi-owner permission arrangement.

## Task tracking, Goals and approvals: How do you manage ongoing tasks?

You can follow Grok's work in conversations, drafts and its computer view. For Muse, look at activity, Goals, Artifacts and approval cards. Cue's published group examples show files moving between roles and decisions returning to the user. Check the deliverable as well as the point where work waits for you.

- Grok Bot: The [public sales demo](https://x.ai/bot) shows a login request, messages from other Bots and drafts before a routine is created. The computer view lets the person watch or take control. The demo's counts and completed actions are example content.
- Meta Muse: [Official UI images](https://introducing.muse.ai/) show activity, Goals and purchase approval cards, with documents and interactive outputs in Artifacts. You can inspect a document in Artifacts or respond to a pending approval card.
- Manus Cue: The [launch example](https://www.manus.im/blog/introducing-manus-2-0) describes agents researching venues, making a shortlist and preparing a deck. The user makes the final call. Its published group image also shows files and a booking agent promising confirmation before payment.

## Which personal agent should you choose?

- Try Grok Bot when **persistent roles and a shared Team Bot** fit how your colleagues work.
- Try Meta Muse for **ongoing goals across supported personal or business tools**, with goals, activity and approvals to review.
- Look at Manus Cue when **separate agent identities and group handoffs** are central to the task.

Already using Claude Code or Codex? Read [Personal agents vs coding agents](/blog/personal-agents-vs-coding-agents) to check whether adding a personal-agent product would address a gap in your current setup.

If your bot already works with your files and tools, [Alook](https://alook.ai/) lets **colleagues and their bots join the conversation**. Keep a supported execution tool, such as Claude Code or Codex, so a coworker can ask the bot about its work. Each owner controls their runtime and tools, but you need to set up the connection and leave the execution machine online. We have not established native Alook connections to Grok Bot, Muse or Cue. See the [coworker example](/blog/ask-coworker-codex-claude-code) for that interaction, or the [Grok Bot comparison](/blog/alook-vs-grok-bot) for the two-product decision.
