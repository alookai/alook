For personal admin, you might ask Muse to pull dates from school emails into your calendar and track a recurring shopping list. A one-off research question can go in a side chat while those tasks continue.

Muse also has a business case. Meta lists connectors including Shopify, QuickBooks, Slack and Notion. For a shop owner, customer records and campaign drafts can fit in the same setup.

For a purchasing role in Grok Bot, Bot email gives the agent a way to request a supplier quote. Payment access can then support the order, subject to your approval rules. That is a different brief from a research Bot producing your weekly competitor report.

For a travel goal in Cue, one agent can contact venues while another organizes the itinerary. Their separate contributions come back to the group, where the next agent can pick up the work.

## Weekly Usage Limits: Upgrade or Split the Work?

Your allowance has to cover the finished job, including corrections. The number of chat messages tells you little about a task that opens websites, reads files and retries failed steps.

For a weekly research brief, a shortlist has to include sources you can follow and facts you can use. A cheap first draft can become expensive if it needs several more runs. Setup and experimentation belong in the same budget as the recurring work.

With Grok’s current shared pool, small lookups and work elsewhere in the bundle can consume allowance needed for a deadline. A narrower recurring routine is easier to budget before moving to a bigger plan.

One way to divide the allowance is to keep light personal tasks separate from demanding work routines. Muse can handle daily admin while Grok Bot keeps a defined work role. You assign work to each app separately and carry over any result the next job needs. The demanding routine can then have its own budget.

## Multi-Agent Collaboration and Team Sharing

Grok Bot and Cue show named agents handing work to one another in a conversation. Muse uses internal subagents to carry out work, including browser tasks. For a team, check who can join and which connected accounts they can use.

Grok’s shared Bots let colleagues use the same configured role. They are available to Cursor team members with Grok Bot access. Sharing opens the same Bot to teammates, while each person’s private conversation stays private. It can also participate in Slack.

The owner’s configured tools and secrets become available through that Bot to the people it is shared with, so only add accounts and files suitable for their use. Replies draw on the allowance of the person the Bot answers. Team-seat costs are separate from the individual prices above.

Cue’s group chat is a more direct way to divide a goal among named agents. Its published travel example shows an itinerary, a packing list and a booking handoff. The deliverables stay visible beside the conversation that moves them to the next agent.

## Computer Access, Permissions and Payment Approvals

Decide which purchases and outbound messages must wait for your approval.

Grok Bot’s approval rules let you specify those actions. A saved payment card does not guarantee a new confirmation at every checkout. Running commands on your own desktop has a separate permission, with an ask-each-time default.

Muse uses a separate permission system called Sentinel. It can allow, deny or ask based on the action and the permissions already granted. When an action requires approval, execution stops while Muse waits for an answer. Previously permitted or low-risk work can continue without another prompt, so the duration and scope of a permission matter.

## What to Do When an AI Agent Gets Stuck

An exhausted allowance, an expired login and a website blocking automation need different responses. Repeating the same request can waste usage without removing the obstacle.

An allowance stop is a spending decision: wait for the reset or pay to finish the remaining work. Login and verification interruptions need a brief handover to you. A blocked checkout can still leave you with a researched shortlist and a purchase to complete yourself.

## Which AI Agent Should You Choose?

Split the work when the jobs need different setups: personal follow-ups in Muse, a repeatable work role in Grok Bot, or a goal divided among Cue’s named agents. Switch when the way a product organizes work or handles access keeps getting in your way, even at an allowance you can afford.

If an agent already finishes work you need with the accounts you have set up, compare the price of more capacity with the effort of moving that setup elsewhere. Paying more makes sense when the next tier covers the work at a cost you can justify.
